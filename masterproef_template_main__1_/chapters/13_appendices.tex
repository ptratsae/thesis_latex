\section*{Feature Importance}
\label{app:exp_recruiter_feature_importance}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Feature Importance}

\paragraph{Interaction matrix PCA}
The embedding interaction matrix $\mathbf{m}_{uj} \in \mathbb{R}^{2d_{\text{text}}}$ has 200 dimensions. \gls{PCA} is applied to reduce the interaction matrix to $d_{\text{pca}} = 80$ components, retaining 92.9\% of the total variance. This compression is justified on two grounds. First, the Hadamard product and absolute difference of the same pair of embeddings share significant covariance structure, meaning a large fraction of the 200 dimensions are redundant — a finding confirmed by the high variance retention at only 40\% of the original dimensionality. Second, reducing the feature space lowers the risk of XGBoost fitting to noise in the interaction dimensions, even with 5,896 training pairs.

\paragraph{Feature importance analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:recruiter_feature_importance} shows the normalised feature 
importance for each of the three base classifiers on the last cross-validation 
fold. The \gls{PCA} interaction components dominate across all models — 
accounting for 69.2\%, 83.2\%, and 85.6\% of total importance in Logistic 
Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost respectively. This confirms that the 
element-wise interaction between user and job Qwen embeddings captures the 
majority of the discriminative signal, validating the design choice of computing 
the full interaction matrix rather than relying solely on scalar cosine similarity.

Among the scalar features, the pattern differs by model family. In Logistic Regression, the Qwen cosine similarity (12.6\%) is the second most important feature, suggesting the linear model benefits from the direct scalar similarity signal. In Random Forest and XGBoost, \texttt{tfidf\_overlap} ranks higher than cosine similarity, suggesting that tree-based models extract more value from explicit keyword matching once the dominant PCA signal is accounted for. \texttt{TotalYearsExperience} ranks consistently across all three models. Both \texttt{degree\_match} and \texttt{full\_profile\_cosine\_sim} contribute negligibly across all classifiers --- under 2.6\% in the best case, and zero in XGBoost --- indicating they are subsumed by the PCA interaction and TF-IDF features. Despite their low importance, both features are retained in the final model. XGBoost's built-in regularisation already suppresses low-importance features during training, so removing them would not meaningfully change performance. Retaining them also preserves consistency with the feature engineering pipeline described in Section~\ref{sec:recruiter_features} and avoids the need for a separate retraining run to confirm that the delta is negligible.

\section*{Hybrid Congestion Ablation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Hybrid Congestion Ablation}
\label{app:hybrid_ablation}

This appendix reports the full four-universe ablation for the hybrid balancing
experiment introduced in Section~\ref{sec:exp_hybrid_results}. The two
intermediate universes --- job-side sigmoid only (Universe~2) and candidate-side
sigmoid only (Universe~3) --- isolate the contribution of each penalty
independently and provide the mechanistic explanation for why the full dual-sided
system (Universe~4) behaves as it does.

Tables~\ref{tab:app_hybrid_job} and~\ref{tab:app_hybrid_candidate} extend
Tables~\ref{tab:hybrid_job} and~\ref{tab:hybrid_candidate} with all four
universes. A structural property is immediately visible in
Table~\ref{tab:app_hybrid_job}: Universes~1 and~3 are identical on the job side,
as are Universes~2 and~4. This is by design --- the candidate-side penalty
operates exclusively in Phase~2 and has no influence on which jobs users apply
to in Phase~1. Consequently, any configuration that shares the same job-side
strategy (penalty active or not) will produce the same job distribution,
regardless of what happens in Phase~2. All four universes differ on the candidate
side (Table~\ref{tab:app_hybrid_candidate}), since both the job-side penalty
(through its indirect effect on inbox composition) and the candidate-side penalty
(through direct re-ranking suppression) independently shape the shortlist
distribution.

\subsection*{Job-side penalty only (Universe~2)}

Adding the job-side sigmoid penalty reduces the job Gini substantially from 0.9182
to 0.7130 and grows the safe zone from 11.1\% to 34.0\%. However, this improvement
is considerably smaller than in the standalone job-side experiment, where the same
sigmoid penalty achieved a Gini of 0.5741. The gap is explained by the structure
of the combined scoring function (Equation~\ref{eq:combined_score}): the job
penalty $\delta_\text{job}$ acts only on the Two-Tower component, which carries
weight $\alpha = 0.5$. The recruiter probability term is congestion-unaware and
pulls the combined score in the opposite direction --- a user may still apply to
a congested job if their recruiter score is high enough to outweigh the penalty.
This dilution is a fundamental consequence of the bilateral design: the recruiter
signal improves match quality but attenuates the market-maker's redistribution
power. A higher $\alpha$ would strengthen the penalty at the cost of recruiter
influence, representing a tunable trade-off left for future work.

The candidate-side effect of the job penalty is pronounced: starved candidates
drop from 32.1\% to 6.4\% and the candidate Gini falls from 0.6326 to 0.5253.
This improvement is entirely indirect --- by redistributing applications more
evenly across jobs, each job inbox contains a more diverse set of candidates,
so recruiter attention naturally spreads. The trade-off is a rise in congested
candidates from 6.7\% to 13.9\%: when the job penalty redirects applications
toward previously empty jobs, those jobs now have small inboxes where all
applicants are automatically shortlisted, including candidates already frequently
shortlisted elsewhere.


\subsection*{Candidate-side penalty only (Universe~3)}

The candidate-side penalty applied without any job penalty produces a more modest
improvement: the candidate Gini drops from 0.6326 to 0.5765 and starved candidates
from 32.1\% to 25.8\%. The job-side distribution is unchanged from the bilateral
baseline by design, and the match quality scores are equal for the same reason.
The candidate-side improvement here is smaller than the job-side penalty's indirect
effect on candidate congestion (Gini 0.5765 vs.\ 0.5253 in Universe~2), confirming
that redistributing the application pool in Phase~1 is a more powerful lever for
candidate equity than re-ranking within fixed inboxes in Phase~2.
