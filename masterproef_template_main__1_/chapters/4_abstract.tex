Job platforms suffer from severe market congestion, where popular postings attract disproportionate applications while qualified candidates go unseen. This dissertation addresses this imbalance by proposing a dual-phase AI-driven recommendation framework for two-sided job markets that reduces congestion while preserving fair and efficient matches. On the user side, a Two-Tower neural architecture generates personalised job recommendation lists for jobseekers; on the recruiter side, a lightweight XGBoost model scores candidate suitability for shortlisting. Because recruiter training labels were unavailable, a large language model is used to synthesise them, producing a compact model suited for fast inference. Both components are integrated into a hybrid congestion-balancing strategy that applies job-level score adjustments at recommendation time and candidate-level adjustments during recruiter shortlisting. The approach is evaluated via counterfactual simulations on real application logs from the CareerBuilder dataset, using applications-per-job and shortlists-per-candidate distributions, Gini coefficients, and model-based match probabilities as metrics. The full hybrid strategy reduces the job-side Gini coefficient from 0.918 to 0.728 and lowers the fraction of starved jobs from 85.6\% to 67.4\%, while simultaneously reducing the candidate-side Gini from 0.633 to 0.495 and the fraction of unseen candidates from 32.1\% to 3.9\%.
